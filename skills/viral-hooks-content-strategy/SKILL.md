# Viral Hooks + Content Strategy Skills File

## Purpose
Use this document to:
- Generate high-performing hooks
- Create social content ideas
- Write scripts for short-form video (TikTok, Reels, LinkedIn)
- Maintain consistency in tone and positioning

Primary niche:
AI + Motherhood + Business

---

# 1. Core Content Buckets

## Educational
Teaches something practical or useful.

Examples:
- How to use AI to plan your week
- 3 workflows that save 2 hours a day
- AI tools every founder should know

---

## Entertaining
Engages, amuses, or holds attention.

Examples:
- POV: trying to work during nap time
- Expectation vs reality of working with kids
- Relatable chaos moments

---

## Inspirational
Motivates or shifts mindset.

Examples:
- Building a business with a newborn is possible
- You don't need more time, you need systems
- My journey from overwhelmed to structured

---

## Personal / Story
Builds connection through real life.

Examples:
- Day in the life of a founder mum
- Postpartum reflections
- Behind the scenes of building

---

## Authority / Proof
Builds credibility and trust.

Examples:
- Case studies (clients, workshops)
- Results from AI training
- "After working with X, I noticed…"

---

## Target Content Mix

- Educational: 40%
- Personal: 20%
- Inspirational: 20%
- Authority: 10%
- Entertaining: 10%

# 3. The Viral Scripting Framework (5-Part Structure)

**SCRIPT STRUCTURE — 60-second yap format.**
When scripting, use bullet points per section not full scripts. Bullets are the prompt, delivery is yours.

| Part | Timing | Function | Viewer question answered |
|------|--------|----------|--------------------------|
| Hook | 0-3s | Call out the right person, open a loop | Why should I care? |
| Context | 3-10s | Set the stage, establish credibility | How did we get here? |
| Tension | 10-40s | Expand the pain, layer it, read their mind | What's not working / at stake? |
| Shift | 40-50s | 1-2 sentences that reframe the problem | The realization that changed things |
| Payoff | 50-60s | Switch from I to you/we, give them the lesson | What this teaches THEM |

## Key Rules

- Tension = 60% of the script. Most creators bail too early. Don't.
- Name the symptom first, then the root cause in the tension section.
- The shift should be the most quotable moment in the video. Write it last.
- Payoff belongs to the audience. If you're still saying I here, rewrite it.
- Hook types to rotate: negative (don't do X without...), identity (anyone else feel like...), quantitative (I challenged myself to...)
- The best yaps look unscripted. They're not. They're structured.

## Worked Example 1 — Using AI as a working mum

- **Hook:** Nobody told me AI would become my childcare strategy. But here we are.
- **Context:** On maternity leave, newborn and toddler, still running two businesses, 20-minute work windows max.
- **Tension:**
  - Thought productivity needed long uninterrupted blocks
  - Without that, either rushed everything or did nothing
  - Guilt of being nowhere fully — not with baby, not with work
  - System wasn't designed for mums who are also building
- **Shift:** The problem was never my time. Needed a system that works in fragments.
- **Payoff:** AI picks up where you left off. 20-minute windows and no sleep is not a limitation. It's actually enough.

## Worked Example 2 — Islamic hypnotherapy track with AI

- **Hook:** Made a hypnotherapy track with AI because mainstream ones didn't fit.
- **Context:** Wanted sleep and anxiety help. Found content. None of it landed.
- **Tension:**
  - Wellness content isn't neutral
  - Loaded with assumptions about values and what peace looks like
  - When God is central to your calm, you spend the whole track translating
  - Can't let go if you're in your head the whole time
- **Shift:** AI let me build something in my own language, rooted in my faith.
- **Payoff:** You don't have to use what wasn't made for you. Build what fits.

# 4. Lauren Dudley Patterns

Imported from `lauren-dudley-patterns.md` (analysis of all 46 reels on @bylaurendudley,
7 May – 3 Jul 2026, during her growth from 4.5k to 57k followers).

Her ICP is personal-branding female founders. **That is not Arfah's ICP** (the mum
rebuilding her work life — UK, 28–38, ADHD, AI-curious). Only structural mechanics that
work regardless of topic were kept. Section 4.10 lists what was deliberately left out —
read it before importing anything else from her.

**Runtime guardrail:** her tactical videos run 100–170s. Do not copy that. Arfah's own
642-video dataset says 30–45s hook-driven and 60–90s story formats win for her audience.
Import the structure, not the runtime.

---

## 4.1 Hook family: the stuck-state call-out

The strongest cluster in her data (~50k avg plays). Name the viewer's exact situation in
second person, then promise the exact way out. The promise is always concrete — "this is
exactly what we're going to do" — never vague.

Formula: `If you're [specific stuck state], [concrete promise].`

Examples in Arfah's lane:
- If you're opening ChatGPT and closing it again without typing anything, this is exactly what to type instead.
- If you've been told to "just use AI" and it's made you feel further behind, I have an alternative.
- If your to-do list has had the same five things on it for three weeks, this is the actual problem.
- If you're building something while on maternity leave and everyone keeps telling you to rest, this one's for you.

**Other clusters that transferred:**

| Cluster | Mechanic | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Contrarian challenge | Attack a piece of advice the audience has been given, then rebuild it | Her #1 video, 135k. High reach, lower comment intent |
| Receipts reveal | Transparency with real numbers | Highest like-to-view ratio on her grid (6% vs 3.5% norm) |
| Conditional hypothetical | "If I was starting from zero tomorrow, here's exactly how I'd action week one" | Resets authority for beginners without condescension |
| Raw situational moment | Low-effort, high-relatability, 20–30s | Pace-changer between heavy posts, roughly 1 in 6 |

**A hook only buys the second line.** Her words: "your hook is just a way to get people to
stop scrolling, and if your next sentence is rubbish, what was the point." Write the hook
and the follow-through as a pair, never separately.

---

## 4.2 The credential / journey line — fixed second beat

Beat 2 of every script, 5–15s in. Non-negotiable position. She teaches it explicitly:
"choose a credential line and place it second."

Two valid versions, rotate them:

- **Expert** (teach from results): "I've built two communities from zero — one is now a 1,000-person conference."
- **Journey** (document a direction honestly): "I'm racing my TikTok against my Instagram to 10k by the end of September."

Pick at least one so a stranger knows what they're following for. A journey account can
grow into an expert account.

---

## 4.3 Three hook surfaces

Every video has three separate doors in. Each one calls out the same target person a
different way:

1. **Spoken first line** (0–3s)
2. **On-screen text** (0–3s)
3. **Caption first line**

The caption first line is an *alternative hook*, never a description of the video. Three
chances to make the right viewer feel seen in the first second.

---

## 4.4 One pre-decided CTA per video

Decide the action before scripting, not after. State it as a flat instruction — viewers
are passive, and a clear instruction re-engages the brain. Pick one thing.

- **Save framing:** never "save this for later." Always "save this because you're going to action it." Positions the save as a commitment.
- **Comment-keyword capture** is her engine (comment NEWBIE, comment 50k). Her keyword-CTA videos average 900+ comments vs ~150 without one. Comments feed the algorithm; the DM delivers the asset and starts the funnel.
- **Arfah's equivalent, until a Tier 1 product is live:** waitlist and save. Do not import her high-ticket CTAs (£580 sessions, cohort applications) — there is nothing to sell into yet.

---

## 4.5 Series with a comeback line

Run one idea as numbered episodes, each ending with "follow for episode 2 tomorrow."
Converts a single idea into a week of posts and gives the follow CTA a concrete reason to
exist. Her "five lessons on the road to 50k" ran this way.

---

## 4.6 Caption as the second complete version of the video

The video and the caption are two complete versions of the same asset, not a video plus a
label.

- Restate the full numbered framework in the caption, sometimes expanded. Maximises save utility and catches caption-first readers.
- First line is an alternative hook (see 4.3).
- **The PS block re-introduces and sells.** Nearly every caption ends with "PS. If you're new here, I'm [name], I [credential]…" plus the current offer. New-follower orientation happens in the caption on *every* post, not only in dedicated intro posts.
- Clarification captions harvest comments: posting a correction or expansion of the video in the caption seeds discussion. Her highest-comment video (2,070) does this.

---

## 4.7 Receipts content with real numbers

Transparency with actual figures. Adapted for Arfah from income reveals to
scoreboard and results: race numbers, workshop outcomes, hours saved, what a client
actually shipped. Specific numbers everywhere — counts, amounts, days, deadlines.

---

## 4.8 Public narrative arc with a clock

The single biggest structural finding. Her account runs a public storyline with a
scoreboard (203 followers in January → 50k, with a bet attached), referenced constantly.
The payoff video did 35k plays on 11 seconds of content.

Her four-part test for a narrative arc:

1. It's a **direction**, not a promised outcome.
2. It's **tangible** — a number you can picture the finish line of.
3. It preferably has a **clock** — a deadline gives people a reason to come back.
4. It's **human** — updates, ups and downs, transparency along the way.

Arfah's live arc: **Race to 10k** — TikTok vs Instagram, deadline end of September.
Every applicable video should be able to touch it.

---

## 4.9 Account-level practices

- **Reintroduce your story once a week.** New followers need it, old followers like being reminded why they stayed.
- **Recognisability:** same filming spot, same energy, same edit style, so a returning viewer knows whose video this is before you speak.
- **Instagram is a social graph, TikTok is an interest graph.** On Instagram people follow people, so story and self-reintroduction content is algorithmically load-bearing, not optional. The same video needs more human wrapper on IG than on TikTok.
- **The stuck diagnostic** — a stuck account is failing one of three things, checked in this order: consistency (the floor, not the ceiling — more posts means more data), experimentation (formats, hooks, pacing, topics; whatever works now will eventually stop), humanity (story, opinions, season of life, your face).
- **Trust over information.** Anyone can ask AI and get a better answer free at 3am. The 2026 question is not "does this person know things" but "why trust this person over Claude." Information is free; the human is the differentiator.

# 5. Voice & Tone

Always load the `arfah-voice` skill when generating content.

Requirements:
- Match Arfah's natural tone and writing style
- Keep language simple, direct, and clear
- Avoid generic or "guru" language
- Reflect her lived experience as a founder and mother
- Prioritise honesty and practicality

If there is any conflict between this document and `arfah-voice`, prioritise `arfah-voice`.
